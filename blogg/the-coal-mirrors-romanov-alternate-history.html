The Coal Mirrors: a Romanov alternate history

By Marko Luttinen, In Coetus Salvo

History tells us how the year 1917 ended for the Romanov family. Fiction is allowed to ask a different question: what if someone tried to get them out?

The Coal Mirrors is an English-language novel by Marko Luttinen, published by In Coetus Salvo on 17 October 2026. It is presented as historical suspense and alternate history, set in revolutionary Russia and in British intelligence in 1917. It is suitable for readers aged 12 and up.

This essay stays with what the book already says about itself. The rest belongs in the pages of the novel.

A letter, a mission, 1917

The book page opens with a run of short lines that feel like briefings more than a lecture:

A letter to a king. The beginning of an impossible mission. A fighter from Newcastle. Tristan. Missing cargo. A dangerous discovery. Two recruits. One mission. British intelligence. 1917.

Then the turn that gives the novel its shape: seven out, seven in. Their future unknown. How far would you go to bring them home? Can they escape their fate? The danger they never see.

That is the door into the story. Not a textbook chapter on the revolution, and not a promise that history will be rewritten for comfort. It is a mission with a cost.

Seven out, seven in

The official description is plain about the bargain at the heart of the book. Seven people are to be saved. Seven others take their place.

Tristan, a seventeen-year-old boxer from Newcastle, and Kristina, a young clerk at the War Office, are recruited for a secret British mission: bring the Romanov family out of revolutionary Russia. To give the fugitives a chance, seven doubles must remain behind. They have learned the gestures, voices and habits of an imperial family. Now they must live under guard as the people they have been trained to become.

The escape, for Tristan and Kristina, demands more than false papers and careful preparation. Winter, hunger and injury wear down the family they are trying to protect. Each border brings another uncertain contact, another journey to arrange, another reason to fear discovery. A German officer follows their trail, determined to turn the capture of the Tsar into his own triumph.

As trust grows between the two young agents, they must decide how much of themselves they can give to the mission, and how to live with the price paid by those left behind.

The novel describes itself as a story of wartime espionage, courage and loyalty, following two young people caught between a family’s hope of survival and the violence of a changing world.

Alternate history, not a history lesson

Alternate history starts from a point we recognise and then changes a choice, a rescue, a single night. The Coal Mirrors does not ask the reader to forget 1917. It asks what might have happened if a British mission had tried to take the Romanov family out, and if seven other people had stayed in their place.

That distinction matters for anyone coming to the book from an interest in the period. This is not a claim about what occurred. It is a work of fiction about the cost of attempting to change the course of history, as the book itself puts it.

Readers who want a closer look at the atmosphere can also find illustrations on the book page. They are scenes from the world of the novel, not a separate edition: a letter, Tristan, Kristina, training, the exchange, an escape, the sea. There is a book trailer there as well.

Who the book is for

The Coal Mirrors is written in English. On the site it is aimed at readers of historical suspense and alternate history. The suggested age is 12 and up.

Because Tristan is seventeen in the official description, and because the mission involves winter, hunger, injury and a pursuit that could end in capture, many younger readers will want some stamina, and some may prefer to read it with an adult nearby. Older readers of wartime fiction and spy stories should feel at home in the material, even though the book is not reserved for them alone.

If you are still deciding, the questions on the book page are a fair test. How far would you go to bring them home? And can they escape a fate the danger does not announce in advance?

Where to find it

The full description, formats and links are on the page for The Coal Mirrors. The Kindle edition is listed under ASIN B0HND6MM49 and is available on Amazon.com. The print edition has ISBN 978-9199202921.

Marko Luttinen is a teacher and author based in Sweden. In Coetus Salvo is the press that brings together his books and interactive stories. His other titles include the Swedish fantasy novel Svarta pilarna and the Swedish adventure Blå musslan, both also suitable from age 12. An overview is on the books page.

If a Romanov alternate history set in 1917 is what you came looking for, the next step is the book itself.
